The calculus behind the demo

Read the specification ↗

The specification owns the laws. The implementation applies them; this page explains the result. Supplied strategy bindings, reference parameters and evaluator choices remain explicit.

TC-MODEL-01 What exists, and what can compose?

Brent and WTI reference exposures have identities, signed barrel quantities and USD price terms. A strategy owns direct legs plus optional children. Aggregation counts each leg once; a hedge changes the combined exposure without erasing separate contractual obligations.

book = direct positions + sum(child positions)

The historical adapter represents linear spot-reference exposure. Physical delivery and a futures contract require their own lawful deal models.

TC-EVIDENCE-01 What could the decision know?

Only the preceding observations, existing holdings, prior capital and completed outcomes are available at selection. A missing estimate stays unavailable. After the current mark is revealed, it becomes evidence for the next cycle.

decision(t) uses K(t−1); outcome(t) enters K(t)

The source contains downloaded daily observations, without original publication vintages. This establishes the demo’s computational timing boundary.

TC-GAIN-01 Why choose a trade, a hedge or a smaller book?

The internal-good reference defines a desired region. Each alternative retains its expected-net, tail-risk, cost and resource changes. The simple evaluator first prefers lower normalized deviation, then higher return/risk preference. It recalculates after each composition.

preference gain = ΔE[net P&L] + λ × tail-risk reduction

A negative expected hedge payoff can improve the whole book. The reference supplies desirability; historical observations supply estimates. Neither admits a strategy.

TC-RISK-01 How do tolerances constrain the proposal?

Joint historical moves estimate tail loss. Independent shocks test changes absent from that sample. The supplied policy includes turnover cost, a three-day funding allowance and known-capital bounds when selecting the largest feasible scale.

tail + costs ≤ bound; stress + costs ≤ bound; notional ≤ bound

Insufficient history blocks entry. Exits and explicit scheduled closure remain available, with unavoidable exit costs disclosed. These are experimental inputs and declared adapter rules.

TC-ACTION-01 What does “actioned” mean here?

The selected quantities are fixed before the next mark. At that mark, the replay assumes complete fills and charges turnover. This moves the simulated book into a new state; there is no broker connection or implicit authorization.

quantity change = selected quantity − previous holding

The separate fill-evidence experiment shows why a real execution adapter must preserve unresolved fills.

TC-PNL-01 Why did this outcome occur?

The previous holding earns the move into the current observation. New fills establish exposure for subsequent moves. Net P&L subtracts execution cost and elapsed-day funding; realized plus unrealized reconciles to cumulative economic P&L.

net = qB(previous) × ΔBrent + qW(previous) × ΔWTI − costs − funding

Compare the prior VaR with gross holding loss on its matching horizon. A newly selected book’s expected return concerns the next holding interval. Outcome evidence feeds the next decision without rewriting the earlier forecast.

TC-SENS-01 Where do the Greeks fit?

Greeks are derivatives of valuation: delta measures price exposure, gamma its curvature, vega volatility sensitivity, theta elapsed-time sensitivity and rho interest-rate sensitivity. They depend on the book, factors and valuation model.

dV = ΔBrent × dBrent + ΔWTI × dWTI

This model’s delta equals signed barrel exposure and price gamma is zero. Vega, theta and rho require richer valuation models. The table is recalculated for every observation and proposed book stage. Sensitivities explain exposures; the gain vector compares the desirability of changing them.

Model boundary & evidence provenance

What this experiment can establish

Typed model composition, causal replay under a stated timing model, P&L reconciliation, costs, cash-path risk and evidence-sensitive decisions.

Both strategy and risk control receive earlier observations. The selected exposure executes at the next available mark. An explicit end date schedules the final close; merely truncating the data never does.

What remains unproven

Profitable execution in a live market. These are spot-reference simulations with user-selected parameters; delivery, futures rolls, borrow availability and market impact are outside this replay.

Original data-release vintages are unavailable. Curves are generated examples, and their values are not historical forward quotes.
